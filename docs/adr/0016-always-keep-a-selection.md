# Always keep a selection

`selection.selectedPhotoUuid` is null only when no photo passes the current filters. Whenever the filtered set is non-empty and the selection isn't in it — a filter change knocked it out, or there was never one — the app auto-selects: `droppedUuid` if that photo is back in the set, otherwise `filteredPhotos[0]`, which is the oldest because `loadPhotos` sorts by date at load. A selection that survives a filter change is kept, with one exception that outranks it: a displaced photo coming back wins over whatever was auto-selected in its place. Without that precedence the memory would never pay out, because the replacement almost always survives the widening too. `droppedUuid` holds whatever the last filter change displaced and is cleared by any user-initiated `select`, so flicking a solo toggle off and on returns you to the photo you were on instead of stranding you on the oldest one. The distinction that makes this readable is `select` (the user chose) versus `autoSelect` (the app chose): only the former clears `droppedUuid`, only the latter moves the camera.

The camera rule is one sentence: an auto-select fits the filtered set, a survival doesn't move. Fitting rather than flying to the chosen photo is what makes that possible: the fit reserves room for the popup via `computeTopPadding`, so the popup's own `calculatePanOffset` returns `(0,0)` and stands down. Two orderings have to hold for that to be true, and both are enforced rather than hoped for. The fit is deferred two frames so the popup exists to be measured, and `pan.ts` waits out an in-flight camera animation before measuring anything — a rect read two frames into a `fitBounds` describes where the popup is passing through, not where it will land, and acting on it both panned to the wrong place and cut the fit short via `map.stop()`. That was a real flake, not a hypothetical: the opening camera settled on a different centre depending on whether photos arrived before or after `<map-fit>` mounted. It also means search and Reset stop being special cases: they're filter changes that happen to widen the set, so `selectFirst`, `triggerPostFitActions` and `toggleOldestNewest` all delete. No loss — `toggleOldestNewest` scanned for an extreme that `filteredPhotos` already had at index 0, and scanned wrong, comparing raw `p.date` strings with no timezone normalisation and treating undated (`''`) as oldest where `sortByDate`'s `￿` key sorts it last.

The invariant makes the popup undismissable, which is the point: Escape and empty-map click stop clearing it, and flows.md's dismiss chain ends at the lightbox. Considered and rejected: a soft rule that auto-selects but still allows null (keeps Escape working, but leaves the app with nothing to describe exactly when you've just filtered); splitting a never-null cursor from a dismissible popup boolean (buys back Escape at the cost of a second concept, and `isPopupOpen`'s placement carve-out already shows how that state behaves); and scoping candidates to the viewport rather than the filtered set, which reads well until panning starts changing the selection out from under you. Out of scope: `getEffectiveCoords` conflates "no location" with `0,0`, so an auto-select in a `None`-only filter lands at null island. That's a pre-existing modelling problem this change makes louder rather than causes, and excluding unplaced photos from auto-select would break the workflow the app exists for.

> **Amended: Escape hides the popup.** The invariant itself is untouched — the selection is still never idly null, still auto-selects, still drives the camera by the rule above — but the third paragraph over-claimed its consequence. It reasoned about _dismissal_ and never about _occlusion_: the card is 320px of map you cannot see, sometimes the exact ground you are reading under the pin. Escape's bottom rung now hides it, and anything that asks to see a photo reveals it again — any change of selection, user-chosen or auto, and Space, which steps to the card rather than past it to the lightbox so that no key acts on a photo you cannot see. There is no state to strand yourself in, and "nothing to describe" stays impossible. Empty-map click still does nothing; flows.md's dismiss chain gains the popup as its last entry, the one Escape hides rather than dismisses.
>
> This is not quite the alternative rejected there. The flag sits beside `droppedUuid` in `@common/selection`, where every way a selection changes is already known, and stays out of `isPopupOpen` — so `<map-markers>` and the projection-remount path never learn about it, and the selected marker stays lit while the card is away, which is what tells you the selection survived. The predicted cost is real but small and paid in one place: exiting placement no longer unconditionally returns you to the popup, since placement targets the photo you already had and so never reveals it.
